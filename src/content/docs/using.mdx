kakemono runs in the background. You talk to it through its icon: in the menu bar on a Mac, in the system tray on Windows and Linux. Click the icon to see what it can do:

- **Next** sets a new wallpaper.
- **Pause** stops the timer, if you turned it on.
- **Favorite** keeps the current wallpaper.
- **Show Picker** opens a strip of pictures to choose from.
- **Settings** opens the window with every option.

## Get a new wallpaper

Click the kakemono icon, then **Next**. kakemono downloads a picture from one of your [sources](/sources/) and puts it on your desktop.

New installs never change the wallpaper on their own. To change it on a timer, see [Change it every 30 minutes](#change-it-every-30-minutes).

## Choose one from the picker

Click the icon, then **Show Picker**. A strip of pictures opens along the bottom of your screen. The [home page](/) has a demo of it.

- Click a picture to make it your wallpaper.
- Or use the arrow keys to move, and press **Enter** to set it.
- Press **F** to add the picture to your favorites.
- Press **Esc** to close the strip.

The tabs on the left side of the strip switch between new pictures from your sources, ones you already downloaded, your own folders, and your favorites.

## Change it every 30 minutes

Open **Settings** from the icon and tick **Change wallpaper every**. You can change the 30 to any number of minutes or hours.

Other options on the same screen:

- **Start at login** starts kakemono when you turn on your computer.
- **Change wallpaper on start** sets a new picture each time kakemono starts.
- **Change wallpaper when the system switches between light and dark** sets a new picture when your computer changes mode, for example at sunset.

## Go back, keep, or throw away

The top of Settings shows the current wallpaper. The buttons under it:

- The arrows go back to an earlier wallpaper, and forward again.
- **Favorite** (the heart) copies the picture to your favorites folder, **Pictures/Wallpapers/favorites**.
- **Move to Trash** removes the picture and sets the next one.
- The folder button shows the picture's file on your computer.

## Only dark pictures, only big pictures

Further down in Settings, under **When possible, use images that…**, you can ask for pictures that are landscape, big enough for your screen, dark or light, or close to a colour you choose. If no picture matches every choice, kakemono uses the closest one, so your wallpaper still changes.

## Updates

When a new version is out, Settings shows a banner at the top. Click **Update**, and kakemono installs it and restarts. If you installed with Homebrew, Scoop or a Linux package, see [Updating](/guides/updating/).

## A keyboard shortcut

kakemono has no shortcut of its own, but you can make one that sets the next wallpaper. **Settings** > **General** > **Shortcuts** shows how on your system.
